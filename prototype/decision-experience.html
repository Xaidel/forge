PROTOTYPE Decision & review experience — what should the developer see when Forge stops?
Prototype notes

What this is testing

What the developer sees when Forge stops for a mid-flight decision, how they review the result, and how they attest it. The surface split is the decision: terminal-only, local web, or both.

The sample task is T-042 · Make webhook delivery idempotent. Forge hits a hard policy trigger (schema / data migration) at step 3 and asks where idempotency keys should live.

A · Terminal-native

Split: plan, decision, permission, review and attestation all live in the terminal.

  • Best at: zero context switch, SSH, speed, scriptability.
  • Weak at: diff review, long rationale, annotation, evidence browsing.
  • Away: state is on disk; closing the terminal is safe. forge resume T-042 reprints the pending gate. No timeout.
  • Risk: the review becomes a pager, not a place to think.

B · Local web cockpit

Split: all four gates live in the local web; the terminal starts the server and shows a log.

  • Best at: annotated diff, evidence, rationale, attestation coaching.
  • Weak at: context switch on every decision; server lifecycle; SSH requires a tunnel.
  • Away: the browser can close and the server can stop. forge resume T-042 reacquires the lock, verifies the worktree, and prints a fresh local URL. Nothing expires.
  • Risk: a browser tab becomes the product, and the terminal becomes a launcher.

C · Hybrid by gate

Split: decisions and permissions in the terminal; plan review, result review and attestation in the local web.

  • Best at: fast decisions in flow, rich review where it matters.
  • Weak at: two surfaces to build and keep in sync; the handoff must be obvious.
  • Away: the terminal keeps the gate receipt; the web room can close. forge resume T-042 restores both. No timeout.
  • Risk: the developer answers in the terminal but reviews in the browser, and the decision record drifts between them.

What to judge

  • Where do you want to decide? Where do you want to review?
  • Is the decision card complete enough to answer without opening the diff?
  • Does the terminal review lose too much? Does the web review make the decision feel too far away?
  • Is the attestation strict enough? Too strict? What should happen after two failed attempts?
  • Does “delegate to Forge for analysis” preserve human ownership, or is it auto-approval in disguise?
  • Is “no default” the right answer when no option is safe?

Open questions for the resolution

  • Surface split: A, B, C, or a variant of C.
  • Delegate: analysis only (Forge researches and re-asks), decision delegated to the agent with constraints, or delegated to a teammate (needs shared state, not v1).
  • Attestation failure: stop after two failed attempts, stay in review with coaching, or allow a “not understood” flag that blocks merge.
  • Away: no timeout (shown) vs. a reminder. Neither may auto-approve.

Prototype constraints

  • Single HTML file; no backend, no persistence; state resets on reload.
  • The Postgres diff is rendered for every choice. Choosing another option records it in state but does not redraw the diff.
  • Attestation grading is simulated: use the “strong / vague / wrong” buttons to see each state. Typing an answer of 40+ characters records it as strong.
  • Arrow keys switch variants when focus is not in a field.
  • Deep links: ?variant=B&flow=review&decided=1 opens a flow directly; add &answered=1 for the final attestation, &done=1 for the done screen, &away=1 for the waiting screen.
